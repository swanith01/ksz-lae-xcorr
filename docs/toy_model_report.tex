\section{Question}
La Plante, Sipple \& Lidz (2022) Fig.~7 plots $D_\ell^{\rm cross}$ (cross-power of the filtered kSZ$^2$ map with a galaxy window of width $\Delta z=1$) against the mean ionised fraction $x_{\rm HII}$ of the window. It rises from $x_{\rm HII}\simeq0$, peaks near $x_{\rm HII}\simeq 0.1$--$0.2$ and has fallen to almost nothing by $x_{\rm HII}\simeq0.8$. Is that shape already explained by a few lines of algebra, and which ingredient makes it?

\section{The model}
\textbf{Estimator.} $S=[F\Theta]^2$ with $\Theta=\sum_i\theta_i$, $\theta_i=-W_i(1+\delta_i)x_{e,i}v_i/c$, $W_i=\tau'(1+z)^2e^{-\tau_i}ds_i$. The toy treats the lightcone as independent slices (the ``diagonal'', square-then-project ordering) and works in the squeezed limit: the small-scale kSZ$^2$ power $s(z)$ of a slice is modulated by the long-wavelength density $\delta_L$ of that slice, $s\to s\,(1+R\,\delta_L)$, and the galaxy map is $g=\int w\,b_g\,\delta_m$.

\textbf{Slice power.} With $K(z)=\tau_{\rm pref}(1+z)^2e^{-\tau(z)}$ and $v(z)=E(z)\,\Omega_m(z)^{0.55}D(z)/(1+z)$ (i.e.\ $aHfD$ in units of $H_0$),
\begin{equation}
 s(z)=K^2v^2\left[\xb^2+\epsilon\,\xb(1-\xb)\right].
\end{equation}
The first term is the kSZ of a uniformly ionised slice with fraction $\xb(z)$ moving with the velocity field. The second is the patchy term: a binary ionisation field has variance $\xb(1-\xb)$, and $\epsilon$ is the ratio of patchy to homogeneous power at the filter scale. \emph{$\epsilon$ is the only shape parameter.}

\textbf{Response to a long mode} ($\partial s/\partial\delta_L=R\,s$):
\begin{align}
 \text{density/velocity:}&\quad (1+\delta)^2\to 2\delta_L \;\Rightarrow\; R_{\rm d}\,s=(2+r_v)\,K^2v^2\left[\xb^2+\epsilon\xb(1-\xb)\right],\quad r_v=0,\\
 \text{ionisation (zreion):}&\quad z_{\rm re}=z_{\rm mean}+(1+z_{\rm mean})\,b_{zm}(k)\,\delta_L(z_{\rm mean}),\\
 &\quad\frac{\partial\xb}{\partial\delta_L}=(1+z_{\rm mean})\,b_{zm}(k)\,\frac{D(z_{\rm mean})}{D(z)}\,p(z),\qquad p=-\frac{d\xb}{dz},\\
 &\quad R_{\rm i}\,s=K^2v^2\left[2\xb+\epsilon(1-2\xb)\right]\frac{\partial\xb}{\partial\delta_L}.
\end{align}
Here $b_{zm}(k)=b_0/(1+k/k_0)^{0.2}$ with $b_0=1/1.686$, $k_0=0.9\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$, $z_{\rm mean}=8$ (La Plante Eqs.~1--3, as in \texttt{zreion/box.py}).

\textbf{Cross-power} (Limber, top-hat galaxy window of width $\Delta z$, $k=\ell/\chi$):
\begin{equation}
 D_\ell=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2\pi}\,\frac{A}{\Delta z}\int_{z_0-\Delta z/2}^{z_0+\Delta z/2}\!dz\;\left(R_{\rm d}s+R_{\rm i}s\right)\,b_g(z)\,\frac{D(z)^2P_{\rm lin}(\ell/\chi)}{\chi^2},
\end{equation}
with $P_{\rm lin}$ Eisenstein--Hu, $b_g=2.1(1+z)-5.3$ (the BlueTides fit used by the repo; $\simeq9.4$ at $z=6$, $22$ at $z=12$) for every tracer, and one overall amplitude $A$ per $\ell$ (it absorbs $T_{\rm CMB}^2$, the filter's share of the slice power and the velocity normalisation). The $x_{\rm HII}$ of a window is $\langle\xb\rangle$ over it. Simplification: the power is taken per unit redshift; the factor $d\chi/dz$ is omitted (including it leaves the best $\epsilon$ unchanged, misfit $0.33\to0.43$).

\textbf{History.} La Plante's digitised fiducial history, made monotonic, Gaussian-smoothed ($\sigma_z=0.3$) so that $p=-d\xb/dz$ is usable, with $\xb=1$ below the table and an $e^{-(z-11.86)}$ tail above its end ($\xb=0.06$). $\tau(z)=\int\tau_{\rm pref}\,\xb\,(1+z)^2d\chi$ from $z=0$.

\section{Fit}
For each $\epsilon$ and each $\ell\in\{500,1000,3000\}$ the amplitude $A$ is the log-space least-squares value against La Plante's digitised Fig.~7 curve over $x_{\rm HII}=0.1$--$0.9$ (40 points); the misfit is the rms of $\ln D_{\rm LP}-\ln(A\,D_{\rm toy})$. $\epsilon$ is the value minimising the misfit averaged over the three $\ell$.

\section{Results}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textbf{Best $\epsilon\simeq8$} (misfit 0.28/0.34/0.36 in log at $\ell=500/1000/3000$; $A=7.0,3.4,1.7\times10^{6}$). Peak at $x_{\rm HII}\simeq0.24$ (La Plante: 0.12--0.19); falls to $\sim0$ by $x_{\rm HII}\simeq0.8$. The shape is therefore reproduced to within the digitisation and the toy's crudeness; the peak sits slightly too late.
\item For $\epsilon\lesssim3$ (homogeneous velocity term competitive) the misfit is $\gtrsim0.7$: the peak moves to $x_{\rm HII}\simeq0.3$ ($\epsilon=3$), 0.55 ($\epsilon=1$), 0.7 ($\epsilon=0$) and the curve keeps 30--70\% of its peak value at $x_{\rm HII}=0.9$, whereas La Plante keeps $\sim2\%$ (toy at $\epsilon=8$: 6\%).
\item The \emph{density/velocity} part alone peaks at $x_{\rm HII}\simeq0.4$--$0.45$ and cannot fall fast enough. The \emph{ionisation} part alone peaks at $x_{\rm HII}\simeq0.2$ and goes to zero where $2\xb+\epsilon(1-2\xb)$ changes sign, $\xb=\epsilon/[2(\epsilon-1)]\simeq0.57$; this, together with $p\to0$ as reionisation ends, produces the late fall.
\item Robustness: $z_{\rm mean}=7,8,9$ give the same $\epsilon$ and misfit, because $(1+z_{\rm mean})D(z_{\rm mean})$ is nearly constant. The minimum in $\epsilon$ is sharp (misfit 0.33 at 8.25, 0.50 at 10, 1.0 at 11, $\ge1.3$ for $\epsilon\ge12$) because the total goes through zero; $\epsilon$ should not be quoted as a measurement.
\end{itemize}

\section{What it says}
The early rise and the late fall of $D_\ell^{\rm cross}(x_{\rm HII})$ are produced by \emph{patchy} ionisation: the kSZ$^2$ power of a slice that is partly ionised (growing as $\xb(1-\xb)$) is correlated with the large-scale density mainly through where bubbles form (the zreion response $\propto p(z)$), not through the homogeneous velocity term. This is consistent with the component study, where $v_{\rm los}$ and the ionisation morphology both follow the kSZ curve. A tracer that is a better proxy for $\delta_L$ at the patchy epoch (larger effective $b_g$, less noise) therefore carries more of this signal.

\section{Caveats and checks to do}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Squeezed-limit, slice-diagonal toy: no cross-slice pairs, no LOS cancellation of the homogeneous term. $\epsilon\sim8$ may partly stand in for that cancellation, so it is a statement about the relative size of the patchy and homogeneous terms in the \emph{estimator}, not a measured physical ratio.
\item $x_{\rm HII}<0.1$ is controlled by the first points of the digitised history (a small wiggle remains); $b_g(z)$ is one shape for all tracers; only the shape is fitted, the amplitude is not predicted.
\item Not yet done: the same overlay on our own measured curves (\texttt{--ours-root}) and a direct test of $\epsilon$ from the simulation (power of the $x_e$-only versus $v$-only kSZ$^2$ maps per window, scripts 48/49).
\end{itemize}
